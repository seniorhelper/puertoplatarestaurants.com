Eating in Costámbar and Cofresí

Two residential beach communities west of the city with loyal regulars and a handful of long-running restaurants.

Head west from Puerto Plata’s center and the coast road passes Costámbar, a gated residential community with a quiet beach, and then Cofresí, known for its resorts and its beach. Many expats and long-stay visitors live here, and the restaurants reflect that: smaller, often family-run, with regulars who come back every week.

Cofresí highlights

  • Le Papillon in Villas Cofresí is a long-standing dinner restaurant pairing European classics with Caribbean seafood. Seating is limited; reviewers recommend booking.
  • Los Tres Cocos at La Roca has been family-run since 2006 with a Caribbean and international menu.
  • Charros CaribeMex sits by Cofresí beach for Mexican-Caribbean plates and has a family pool, according to its own page.

Costámbar highlights

  • Sole Mio is the local pick for pizza and pasta, tucked inside the community on Calle Anacaona.
  • The beach itself has simple bars and grills that change hands often; we list only what we can verify, so ask locals what is open this season.

Getting there

Both areas are a short taxi ride from the center. Costámbar is the closer of the two. If you are on a ship docked at Amber Cove, these communities sit between the port and the city, which makes them handy for a lunch stop on the way into town; see our cruise-port guide. Evening taxis back to Playa Dorada or downtown are best arranged with the restaurant.

Why come out here

Dinner west of town feels calmer than the Malecón, with more conversation and less traffic noise. It is also where you are most likely to meet people who live on the coast full time, and that is often the best way to hear which comedor or beach shack is cooking well right now.
