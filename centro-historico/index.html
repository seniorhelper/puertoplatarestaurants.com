Eating in the Centro Histórico

Gingerbread balconies, a 19th-century gazebo and some of the most atmospheric dining rooms on the North Coast.

Puerto Plata’s old town is known for Victorian architecture that travel writers often call the finest in the country. Wooden houses with fretwork balconies line the streets around the central park, where a gazebo built at the end of the 19th century still anchors the square. The Amber Museum sits in a Victorian building on Calle Duarte, about a hundred meters from the park, and the cathedral faces the plaza.

Several of those houses now hold restaurants, which makes dinner here feel like a small history lesson. Others are simple lunch counters where office workers and families eat the plato del día.

A walking food route

  1. Start at the central park. Casita Azul, a blue 1879 house on Calle Beller, faces the square. Reserve a table on the porch at sunset.
  2. Turn onto Calle Duarte. Casa 40 is a few steps away in another old house with a back patio; check the day, as it closes once a week.
  3. Walk to Calle Profesor Juan Bosch. La Isabela Colonial Restaurant and Kaffe both occupy restored houses with antiques and art on the walls.
  4. Lunch like a local. Skina is the straightforward option for Dominican plates. Small comedores on side streets serve La Bandera at midday; look for the busiest.
  5. End with a cocktail. Sambalú is the newer, more stylish choice for a drink and a Caribbean-fusion bite.

Comedores and colmados

A comedor is a family-run lunch room with a few set plates. You point at the steam table, choose rice, beans and a meat or fish, and pay in pesos. They open for lunch and sell out by mid-afternoon. A colmado is the corner store-bar where locals buy cold drinks and snacks and often linger with music. Both are part of daily life in the center, and our cheap eats guide explains how to order.

Practical notes

  • The center is compact: you can walk between all the places above in under fifteen minutes.
  • Many old houses are not air-conditioned; evenings are more comfortable.
  • If you arrive by cruise ship at Taíno Bay, the center is walkable. See our cruise-port food plan.

Questions about eating here

Which historic-center restaurant is best for a date?

Casita Azul and La Isabela both occupy restored Victorian houses with a romantic feel. Book ahead on weekends.

Is the historic center walkable from the Malecón?

Yes. The central park is roughly a ten- to fifteen-minute walk from the waterfront.
