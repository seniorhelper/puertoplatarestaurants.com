How the picker works

The picker reads our public restaurant data file, the same one behind the restaurant list. Each restaurant carries editor’s tags drawn from its sources: the area it is in, the kinds of food it serves, and moods such as "sea view" or "historic setting" when a guidebook or the business itself describes them. Choosing an area keeps results there and adds neighboring areas a short ride away; choosing a cruise port shows the areas closest to that pier.

Results are scored, not paid. Matching a craving or mood moves a restaurant up; nothing moves a sponsored business ahead of others. Meal-time tags appear only when a source states them (for example, a dinner-only restaurant), and diet matches appear only when a source confirms a vegetarian focus. If no place has a confirmed tag, the tool tells you so and suggests dishes that are typically meat-free, so you can ask the kitchen.

The "make it an evening" plan builds three stops in the same area: a drink, dinner and a coffee or dessert place, if we have sourced options for each. Your choices are saved in the page address, so you can bookmark or share a result. Nothing you pick is sent to us.

Three example plans

Date night downtown: choose Centro Histórico, International or Seafood, and Romantic. The picker surfaces the Victorian-house restaurants (Casita Azul, La Isabela) and suggests Sambalú for a cocktail first.

Cruise-day lunch: choose "Arriving at Taíno Bay port", Seafood and Sea view. You get Malecón and historic-center places within walking distance, led by La Carihuela Beach and Tam Tam.

Family dinner in the west: choose Cofresí, Mexican or International, and Family. Charros CaribeMex and Los Tres Cocos come up, with Costámbar’s Sole Mio a short ride away.
