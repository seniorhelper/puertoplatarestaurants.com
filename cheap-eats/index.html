Budget eating

Cheap eats in Puerto Plata: comedores, colmados and street food

How locals eat well for little: the plato del día, the corner colmado and the snack stands worth stopping for.

The comedor

A comedor is a small, usually family-run lunch room. There is rarely a printed menu: you walk up to the counter or steam table and choose. The standard plate is La Bandera, white rice with stewed beans and a meat, plus a little salad. Some days you will find locrio, stewed goat (chivo guisado), fried fish or sancocho. Lunch runs from about noon to 2 p.m., and the best comedores sell out early.

How to order: point and say "un plato con arroz, habichuelas y pollo" (a plate with rice, beans and chicken). Ask "¿cuánto es?" before you sit if you want the price first. Pay in pesos.

The colmado

The colmado is part corner shop, part neighborhood bar. You can buy cold drinks, snacks, bread, eggs and a single beer to drink on the spot, often with music playing. Many deliver to nearby homes on a motorbike. For travelers in rentals it is the easiest place to grab basics.

Street food to try

  • Empanadas and pastelitos: fried turnovers with cheese, chicken or beef.
  • Chicharrón: crisp fried pork, with lime and fried yuca.
  • Frituras: fried yuca, plantain, sweet potato or salami from evening carts.
  • Yaniqueques: thin fried dough discs, a beach snack.
  • Fresh juices: chinola, lechosa or a creamy morir soñando.

Staying safe and comfortable

  • Choose stalls with a queue and food cooked to order.
  • Skip raw items from carts; drink bottled or purified water.
  • Carry small notes and coins; change for large bills can be scarce.

A budget day in Puerto Plata

Morning: coffee and a pastelito from a bakery, or mangú at a comedor that opens early. Midday: the plato del día at the busiest comedor you can find, with a fresh juice. Afternoon: a cold drink at a colmado and a walk on the Malecón. Evening: frituras or chicharrón from a cart, or a casual sit-down plate. You will eat well, meet locals and spend a fraction of a resort dinner.

Useful phrases

  • "¿Qué hay hoy?": what is there today?
  • "Para llevar": to take away.
  • "Sin carne, por favor": without meat, please (ask if beans are cooked with meat).
  • "La cuenta, por favor": the bill, please.
  • "¿Aceptan tarjeta?": do you take cards?

Casual sit-down picks

If you want a menu and a table without spending much, Skina downtown is known for big portions of Dominican food, and on the Malecón Big Lee’s keeps bar food cheap. In Costámbar, Sole Mio is a relaxed pizza stop.
