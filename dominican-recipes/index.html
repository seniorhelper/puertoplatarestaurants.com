Classic recipes

Cook a Dominican dish at home: mangú, sancocho, tostones

These are traditional Dominican recipes passed down through generations of Caribbean cooks. If you are staying in a villa or apartment in Puerto Plata, they make an unforgettable evening with ingredients from the market.

Mangú con los Tres Golpes

The Dominican Breakfast of Champions

Mangú is made from boiled, mashed green plantains and is one of the oldest dishes in Dominican culinary tradition, with roots tracing back to African cooking. "Los tres golpes" (the three hits) refers to the three accompaniments: fried salami, fried white cheese, and fried eggs. This recipe serves 4.

Ingredients (Serves 4)

  • 4 green plantains
  • ½ cup cold water
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 white onion, sliced
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 8 oz Dominican salami
  • 8 oz queso de freír (frying cheese)
  • 4 eggs
  • 1 tbsp white vinegar

Method

  1. Peel and boil plantains in salted water for 20–25 minutes until very tender.
  2. Mash plantains with cold water and butter until smooth. Season with salt.
  3. Sauté onion slices in olive oil with a dash of vinegar until golden and slightly caramelized. Set aside.
  4. Fry salami slices and cheese until golden. Fry eggs to your preference. Plate mangú topped with onions, serve los tres golpes alongside.

Sancocho Dominicano

The Seven-Meat Dominican Stew

Sancocho is the Dominican Republic's most celebratory dish — eaten on Sundays, at family gatherings, and at festivals. The traditional version uses seven different meats and a rich collection of root vegetables. This simplified version captures the essential character of the dish.

Ingredients (Serves 6–8)

  • 1 lb bone-in chicken
  • 1 lb beef short rib
  • 1 lb smoked pork sausage
  • 2 green plantains, cut
  • 1 lb yuca (cassava)
  • 1 lb ñame (yam)
  • 1 corn cob, cut in pieces
  • 1 cup auyama (pumpkin)
  • 1 onion + 3 garlic cloves
  • Cilantro, sazón, salt, lime

Method

  1. Marinate all meats overnight with garlic, sazón, oregano, and lime juice.
  2. Brown meats in batches in a large pot. Add onion and garlic; sauté 2 minutes.
  3. Add 10 cups water. Bring to boil, reduce heat, and simmer 45 minutes.
  4. Add root vegetables and plantain. Simmer 30 more minutes. Add pumpkin last 15 minutes. Season and finish with fresh cilantro.

Tostones

Twice-Fried Green Plantains

Tostones are arguably the most addictive thing you will eat in the Dominican Republic. Green plantains sliced, fried once, smashed flat, then fried again until perfectly crispy. Served with garlic sauce or simply with salt. This is the easiest Dominican recipe to master at home.

Ingredients (Serves 4)

  • 3 green plantains
  • Vegetable oil for frying
  • Salt to taste
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • ¼ cup olive oil
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Fresh parsley
  • Cold water (for soaking)

Method

  1. Peel plantains and cut into 1-inch thick rounds. Heat oil to 325°F (163°C).
  2. Fry rounds 3–4 minutes per side until light golden. They should be soft, not crispy yet. Remove and drain.
  3. Smash each round flat using the bottom of a glass or a tostonera. Dip briefly in cold salted water. Pat dry.
  4. Raise oil to 375°F (190°C). Fry smashed tostones 2–3 minutes until golden and crispy. Drain, salt immediately. Serve with garlic-lime sauce.

Shopping in Puerto Plata

Plantains, yuca, auyama and cilantro are sold at markets and colmados all over town; Dominican salami and frying cheese (queso de freír) are in every supermarket. Not sure what to make from your haul? Try the ingredient matcher.
