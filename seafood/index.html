Seafood guide

Seafood in Puerto Plata: what to order and where

A decoder for menu names, how fish is cooked on the North Coast, and the beach and dinner spots that do it best.

Puerto Plata faces the open Atlantic, and fishing boats still work the coast. On menus you will see fish named in Spanish, often without a translation. Use the table below, then ask the server what came in today: "¿Qué hay fresco hoy?"

Menu wordWhat it is
ChilloRed snapper. Usually served whole, fried crisp (chillo frito) or braised a la criolla.
DoradoMahi-mahi. Grilled fillets or in fish tacos at international spots.
MeroGrouper. Firm white fish for grilling and stews.
LambíQueen conch. Stewed (lambí guisado), in salads or fried. Like lobster, it is a regulated fishery.
LangostaSpiny lobster. Grilled with garlic butter. Subject to an annual closed season (veda).
CamaronesShrimp: al ajillo (garlic), al coco (coconut) or in locrio.
PulpoOctopus, often in a vinaigrette salad.
Pescado con cocoFish simmered in coconut milk, a style common in the Samaná region and found on some North Coast menus.

How it is cooked

  • Frito: whole fish scored and deep-fried, served with tostones, rice or fries, salad and lime. The beach classic.
  • A la criolla: braised in tomato-pepper sofrito.
  • Al ajillo: sizzled with garlic and oil.
  • A la plancha: grilled on a flat-top, the lightest option.

Sustainability and seasons

The Dominican Republic has an annual closed season for spiny lobster to protect breeding, and conch is also regulated. If lobster appears during the closed months, ask where it came from, or choose snapper, dorado or shrimp instead. Whole fish is usually priced by weight; ask the price before you order so the bill holds no surprises.

Best places for seafood

For fish with your feet near the sand, go to the Malecón: La Carihuela Beach and Tam Tam are the long-standing choices. For seafood in a candlelit room, try Casita Azul or La Isabela downtown, Le Papillon in Cofresí, or Lucía in Playa Dorada.
