Where to eat in Playa Dorada

The resort complex east of the city mixes hotel fine dining with a shopping plaza full of independent kitchens.

Playa Dorada is the gated resort zone just east of Puerto Plata’s center, built around a long beach, a golf course and a cluster of hotels. Many guests stay all-inclusive, but the area also has independent restaurants that are worth leaving the buffet for, especially around the Playa Dorada Plaza shopping center and El Pueblito.

Who it suits

Choose Playa Dorada when you want variety close to your hotel: Italian seafood, Japanese-Peruvian Nikkei, a casual churrasco, or a candlelit French-Caribbean dinner. Prices run higher than in town, but you avoid a taxi each way. If you are staying in town, the zone is a short ride east.

Highlights

  • Lucía at Casa Colonial is the special-occasion choice. Fodor’s marks it a Fodor’s Choice and says reservations are essential; it serves dinner only.
  • Ristorante Passatore at the plaza handles Italian and seafood.
  • Mikasa brings Japanese and Nikkei cooking, a rarity on this coast.
  • Green Jack is the relaxed option; reviewers rate its churrasco.
  • Le Petit François in El Pueblito does beachfront Caribbean plates and takeout.

Leaving the all-inclusive

Resort wristbands do not cover outside restaurants, so plan to pay as usual. Most places in the zone accept cards, but bring pesos for tips and small purchases. Security gates control car access to the complex; tell the taxi driver the restaurant or hotel name. Sit-down bills add tax and service, so check what is included before adding a tip.

Pairing with the city

A good day plan: morning on the beach, lunch at the plaza, then a taxi into the historic center for an afternoon walk and dinner on the Malecón. Or flip it: explore town by day and come back to Playa Dorada for a reserved dinner.

Casual places usually do. For Lucía, Fodor’s says to reserve, and weekend evenings fill up elsewhere too.
